# My Table Tennis Gear Notes: Blades, Rubbers, and Experiments

This is an evolving notebook for my own equipment experiments. I am keeping the structure loose on purpose so I can add specific setups only after I have spent enough time with them.

## Current setup

My current setup is the baseline I use to judge changes. When I add details here later, I want to include what I noticed in actual practice instead of only listing specifications.

## What I want from forehand

For forehand, I am looking for a setup that lets me create a comfortable arc and controlled spin without feeling like every shot needs perfect timing.

## What I want from backhand

For backhand, consistency and a predictable response matter most. I want enough help for compact strokes while keeping touch shots and blocks manageable.

## Chinese tacky rubbers

Tacky Chinese rubbers are interesting because they ask for deliberate engagement with the ball. I want to keep notes on how they change serve, opening loops, and the amount of effort a stroke needs.

## Tensor rubbers

Tensor rubbers are a useful contrast: livelier response, a different feel in short play, and often a different relationship with the blade. I will track those tradeoffs here rather than treating one style as automatically better.

## Booster / tuning experiments

Any booster or tuning experiment needs patient testing. If I try one, I want to record the setup, the intended change, and whether it made the result easier to control rather than just faster.

## Secondary blades / experimental setups

Secondary blades give me a way to compare feel without dismantling my main setup immediately. This section is for pairings that are still in the trial stage.

## What I want to test next

- Compare a tacky forehand option with a more elastic alternative.
- Test how different blade pairings affect backhand confidence.
- Keep a better record of durability, replacement cost, and availability.
